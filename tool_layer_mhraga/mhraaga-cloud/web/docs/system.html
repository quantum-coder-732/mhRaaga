How the system works

mhRaaga turns a sentence into a raga song, with the words spoken over it. This page walks through it from the click to the sound, then shows the machinery behind it.

The story of one song

  1. You describe it. "A calm morning bhajan to Krishna", or paste your own lyrics. You can pick a language, a lead instrument, a tala and a length.
  2. The brief. A hosted language model (via OpenRouter) turns that into a plan: raga, tala, tempo, mood, language and lyrics. If the model is down, keyword rules still make a plan, so a song is always produced.
  3. The raga and the tala. The engine knows 30 classical ragas, all 72 melakartas and the 16,000 "gopi" ragas (a pair of melakartas, one of three tonics, a janya cut). Each has an ascent, a descent, a vadi and samvadi, and a time of day.
  4. The score. The composer writes the classical form: alap (the drone and free flute), gat (the tabla enters; each cycle opens on sam; the vadi is held; meend and kan ornaments), then a tihai that lands exactly on sam. Lyrics are timed to the phrases.
  5. The feel. Real physics makes it human: a Box2D spring gives rubato, a falling ball gives each note its weight, another spring gives the meend glide. A small GAN offers fresh phrases. Auto-tune pulls every note toward its shruti, and the last note is snapped exactly to Sa.
  6. The instruments. The score is plain JSON. Your device plays it with physical models: plucked-string tanpura, breathy bansuri, reed harmonium, tabla from its modes, a room reverb, human timing and dynamics.
  7. The voice. Each lyric line is spoken by a neural voice model. The audio is made once, stored in S3 and shared by everyone, so the second listener pays nothing and waits nothing. If the voice service is busy, your device's own voice reads that line instead.
  8. The mix. The browser lays the voice over the music, turns the music down under each line, and runs a limiter so nothing clips. Downloads (MP3 or WAV) include the voice.

The machinery

PartWhat it is
CloudFrontThe only public door. Serves the site and audio from S3, forwards /api/* to the API with a secret header. The raw API address answers 403.
LambdaOne Python function: the whole engine, accounts, the studio, the team projects and the voice endpoint. It also holds the Box2D physics engine.
DynamoDBAccounts (scrypt-hashed passwords, daily quota, lockout) and songs.
S3The site, the shared playlist's assets, the song catalogue, the live rules, voice samples and the cached neural voice audio.
SSMThe model key and the signing secrets. None are in the repository.
Strict CSPThe pages run no inline script or style and may only talk to their own domain.

How it improves

An offline "song factory" runs producer agents that pick raga, tala, tempo, lead instrument, theme, tonic and form. Critics score each song, a fair-credit rule splits the score among the agents, and each agent learns which choices work for each language. The best songs fill a catalogue of up to 100. What it does not do

  • The instruments are physical models, not recordings of a real band.
  • The voice speaks the words; it does not sing them to the melody.
  • Composing and speech call paid hosted models, so they are capped per user and per day.
  • The critics are rules, not listeners. Please listen, and tell us what is wrong.
