aRaaga · how it works

A sentence goes in. A raga comes out.

The app you open is only a client. The AI that plans each song lives on Akanade's Pecova music agent; the service in the middle keeps accounts, queues the work, renders the audio and serves it back.

Who talks to whom

What happens when you press Create

  1. The app submits the promptPOST /api/music/generate with your session token. The service saves a job as QUEUED and answers at once, so the page never waits on audio.
  2. The worker picks it up and asks for a briefThe composer client calls Akanade's /api/v1/orchestration/execute asking for the music_generation capability.
  3. Akanade routes it to the Pecova music agentThe agent asks the platform's model (OpenRouter or the SLM tier) for raga, taal, tempo, mood, instruments and lyrics, then clamps the answer to values the renderer knows.
  4. The brief comes back, or a fallback takes overIf Akanade is down or no token is set, keyword rules build a plainer brief. Each track records which path made it.
  5. The renderer plays the briefIt lays a tanpura drone, a tabla pattern in the chosen taal and a lead voice walking the raga's notes, then encodes mp3 and computes a waveform for the player.
  6. The track is saved and the job completesMetadata goes to DynamoDB, audio to disk, the job flips to COMPLETED.
  7. The app notices and plays itIt polls the job every 1.2 seconds, then streams /api/music/audio/… with Range requests.

A job's life

Languages, voices and raga rules

Four languages

English, Hindi, Marathi and Sanskrit. English lines go to Akanade text-to-speech with the signed-in person's token; Devanagari lines use local Piper models (Hindi, Marathi for Marathi and Sanskrit). No Amazon Polly.

Raga grammar, not just a scale

Every raga has an aroha and avaroha, a vadi and samvadi (held twice as long), and a pakad that opens each cycle. A song follows the Hindustani form: alap (free time, drone and lead only), gat (tabla enters, pakad, rise, fall), then a tihai, the signature phrase three times landing on Sa. Rules live in app/raga.py.

Voice and music in time and in key

The drone sits on Sa, the tabla runs at the brief's tempo, and each sung line is padded to a half or whole taal cycle so it starts on the beat. The lead melody fades while a line is sung and answers in the gaps. A stock recording is used only if it is tagged for the same raga.

Akanade is the AI platform

Login is Akanade's (/api/auth/login, role from /api/users/me). The music agent is registered through Akanade's agent API as a webhook; it calls OpenRouter (cheap model, fallback qwen) and records tokens and cost per task.

Costs: admins and senior managers only

/api/admin/costs shows per song and per user: voice characters, LLM tokens, EC2, S3 and CloudFront estimates. Account-wide OpenRouter credits need OPENROUTER_MANAGEMENT_KEY.

Where it runs

A Docker container on the Akanade EC2 behind nginx, fronted by its own CloudFront distribution; audio in S3 bucket algo-raaga-877157050453 (the instance role needs S3 read/write); the Android app is a Flutter build.

Good to know

The instruments are synthesized; the words are spoken-sung by TTS

Audio is an offline synthesizer, raga and taal accurate. Lyrics are shown as text. A neural vocal or text-to-music model could replace the renderer without touching the rest.

The AI is optional at runtime

Without an Akanade token the service still makes music from keyword rules, so the app works fully offline.

One place for the name

The product name lives in music-web/src/brand.ts and the mobile app title.
